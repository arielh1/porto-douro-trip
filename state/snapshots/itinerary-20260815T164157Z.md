# Itinerary — Porto · Douro · Gerês

**Status:** Structurally rebuilt this cycle — now actually ends in Lisbon with a full
campervan coastal leg, Day 6's drive time is fixed, and every day now carries a real
calendar date and weekday (`trip/facts.md` confirmed dates while this cycle was in
progress). Segment order and rhythm are solid; bookings, opening-day checks against the
real weekdays below, and the harvest-day choice are still soft.
**Cycle:** 2 · **Phase:** explore · **Updated:** 2026-08-15
**Dates:** ✅ real — **Friday 11 September 2026 → Sunday 27 September 2026**, 17 days /
16 nights, per `trip/facts.md`. Flight times themselves are still unknown (affects Day 1
and Day 17 timing only).

**Structure:** 2 nights Porto (arrival) + 6 nights Douro (3 Pinhão + 3 Lamego/Régua) +
2 nights Gerês + 1 night Porto (car→van handover) + 2 nights Peniche/Baleal + 2 nights
Ericeira + 1 night Lisbon, flying home from **LIS**, not OPO. This satisfies every hard
constraint in `trip/facts.md` (≥3 nights/Douro base, ≥2 nights/Peniche-Baleal, ≥2
nights/Ericeira) with room to spare — `trip/facts.md` itself now notes 16 nights is
*more* than the "~2 weeks" originally assumed, so this is no longer a tension to flag,
just a fact to build against.

## Day by day

### Day 1 — Friday 11 September — Land, decompress, nothing else
**Base:** Porto, Cedofeita · **Drive:** none · **Pace:** light

- **Morning/afternoon** — Land at OPO (exact arrival time still unknown — see
  `trip/open-questions.md`). Metro Line E (~26–30 min, €2.25) or Uber (~€10–16) into
  Cedofeita. Check in, shower.
- **Lunch** — whatever's open near the hotel; no reservation.
- **Afternoon** — short walk to get oriented. Nothing booked.
- **Evening** — one low-key tasca dinner within walking distance, loosely timed given
  the still-unknown arrival time (e.g. O Buraco).

> **Book ahead:** nothing — deliberately.
> **If it rains:** irrelevant, no outdoor plan today.
> **Why this day works:** zero-ambition arrival day matches "relaxed and balanced" and absorbs
> flight delay/jet lag without any booking at risk.

### Day 2 — Saturday 12 September — Porto: azulejos, books, a bridge
**Base:** Porto, Cedofeita · **Drive:** none (walking + short metro hops) · **Pace:** medium

- **Morning** — São Bento station azulejo panels (free, 5 min) → wander Mercado do Bolhão
  (open Saturdays) → Livraria Lello at a **pre-booked timed slot** (early morning or late
  afternoon to dodge crowds — a Saturday in mid-September is peak-crowd territory, so book
  the earliest or latest slot available).
- **Lunch** — Café Santiago or Lado B, francesinha — do it once, as a cultural checkbox, not a
  daily habit. Expect a queue, worse on a Saturday.
- **Afternoon** — Torre dos Clérigos climb for the panoramic view. Gap block afterward: no
  plan, wander Baixa/Aliados at will.
- **Evening** — Ponte Dom Luís I upper-deck walk at golden hour, sunset from the Ribeira or
  Gaia riverbank, casual dinner nearby.

> **Book ahead:** Livraria Lello timed slot — a Saturday in September is exactly the
> same-day-sellout scenario `research/porto.md` warns about.
> **If it rains:** swap the outdoor bridge/riverbank sunset for Palácio da Bolsa or Igreja de
> São Francisco (indoor, half-day filler either way).
> **Why this day works:** two anchors (Lello, Clérigos), a real gap in between, ends on a free
> river sunset — no reservation-required tasting menu on a jet-lagged night. The "serious
> meal" now lands on Day 11 instead (see below), paired with Gaia, so tonight stays casual.

### Day 3 — Sunday 13 September — Into the Douro
**Base:** Pinhão · **Drive:** ~1h25–1h30 (A4/IP4, toll-free Marão tunnel) · **Pace:** light

- **Morning** — pick up the rental car at **Campanha train station** (not the airport — city
  pickup avoids airport markup and the Porto walking days didn't need a car). **Automatic
  transmission needs to be booked well ahead** — it's a minority of the Portuguese fleet,
  flagged urgently in `trip/open-questions.md`. A Sunday pickup — worth confirming the rental
  desk's Sunday hours at Campanha specifically.
- **Midday** — drive to Pinhão. This is right at the ~90-min travel-day comfort line — no
  detour, no stop planned as a booked anchor, just the drive.
- **Afternoon** — arrive, check in, walk the village, see the tiled Pinhão station panels
  (free).
- **Evening** — dinner at Cais da Foz (riverside, phone reservation recommended).

> **Book ahead:** rental car (automatic, confirm Campanha pickup and Sunday hours), Cais da
> Foz table.
> **If it rains:** no outdoor anchor today besides the village walk — sit it out at the hotel.
> **Why this day works:** one drive, no second commitment stacked on top of a transfer day.

### Day 4 — Monday 14 September — Terraces and a tasting
**Base:** Pinhão · **Drive:** ~20 min each way to the quinta · **Pace:** full (2 anchors, by design)

- **Morning** — Miradouro de Casal de Loivos loop (~5.6 km / ~1h42, easy circular walk to the
  view the BBC named one of the world's six best) — walk **before** any tasting, not after.
- **Lunch** — light, near the quinta or back in Pinhão.
- **Afternoon** — Quinta do Crasto: guided tour + 5-wine tasting (~90 min, budget a 2h block
  door-to-door; hours confirmed 10:00–18:00 daily, no closure day found — a Monday is fine).
  **Drink-and-drive note:** whoever tastes should not be the one driving back — alternate
  which of you is the taster/driver across the two Douro tasting days (today and Day 7), or
  arrange a quinta transfer for this leg.
- **Evening** — free, back in Pinhão.

> **Book ahead:** Quinta do Crasto tasting (email enoturismo@quintadocrasto.pt) — **and the
> harvest/foot-treading question is now sharper, not softer, now dates are confirmed**:
> `trip/facts.md` explicitly rules out Quinta do Tedo's fixed 2026 harvest date (Saturday 5
> September) — it falls **before** this trip even starts (11 September). That leaves **Quinta
> do Seixo's exclusive harvest experience** (hand-picking + lagar foot-treading + tasting +
> chef-led lunch, ~6h, €250pp, no fixed date but needs advance reservation) as the live
> candidate from research so far, and `trip/facts.md` explicitly asks for harvest options
> **running 12–20 September** — this trip's window. Flagging to `scout-douro` to find more
> candidates in that exact window; this is a placeholder standard tasting until Ariel books a
> harvest package (see `trip/open-questions.md`).
> **If it rains:** the walk gets wet but stays easy underfoot; the tasting itself is indoors
> regardless.
> **Why this day works:** exactly 2 anchors, walk-then-taste ordering keeps the drink-drive
> rule clean, real gap in the evening.

### Day 5 — Tuesday 15 September — Light day, river optional
**Base:** Pinhão · **Drive:** none, or a short taxi to the boat dock · **Pace:** light

- **Morning** — no plan. Sleep in, coffee by the river.
- **Midday/afternoon** — optional rabelo boat trip (Pinhão–Tua–Pinhão, ~2h, several daily
  departures) — treat as take-it-or-leave-it, not a fixed anchor.
- **Evening** — free, dinner wherever looks good in the village.

> **Book ahead:** rabelo boat only if doing it — same-week booking is fine, it's not a
> harvest-season bottleneck item.
> **If it rains:** the boat is the one thing that doesn't happen — everything else about the
> day already assumes no fixed plan.
> **Why this day works:** this is the trip's first genuinely empty day, per preferences' "at
> least one empty day per week" — deliberately placed mid-Douro rather than saved for the end.

### Day 6 — Wednesday 16 September — Down to Lamego, honestly timed
**Base:** Lamego · **Drive:** ~32–36 min direct (Pinhão↔Régua↔Lamego corridor) · **Pace:** medium

- **Morning** — check out, drive **directly** to Lamego. **The São Salvador do Mundo miradouro
  stop from the previous draft is dropped** — `research/logistics.md` confirmed it sits off
  the south bank near São João da Pesqueira, not on this corridor, and would have added
  ~40–55 min of real detour driving on top of the direct ~32–36 min leg. If Ariel wants that
  specific viewpoint, it's a better fit as a half-day add-on from this base later (see Day 8).
- **Lunch** — Lamego old town.
- **Afternoon** — Sanctuary of Nossa Senhora dos Remédios (the baroque staircase + hilltop
  views), wander the old town.
- **Evening** — settle into the Lamego/Régua base, dinner in town (Castas e Pratos, Régua, as
  a candidate — not independently confirmed yet).

> **Book ahead:** nothing hard-required; a Lamego dinner reservation is a nice-to-have in
> September.
> **If it rains:** swap the sanctuary steps for the cathedral/museum interior in Lamego.
> **Why this day works:** the drive is now honestly ~32–36 min (corrected from a previous
> draft's underestimated and mis-routed figure), which comfortably clears the ~90-min
> guideline with lunch + sanctuary + wander still fitting a real "medium pace" day.

### Day 7 — Thursday 17 September — The long lunch
**Base:** Lamego · **Drive:** ~15 min each way · **Pace:** medium (1 real anchor)

- **Morning** — free, wander Lamego or sleep in.
- **Midday/afternoon** — Quinta da Pacheca (Cambres): tour + tasting + lunch, the trip's
  designated "long, unhurried lunch." Same drink-and-drive rule as Day 4 — alternate
  taster/driver from Day 4, or use a transfer for this leg specifically.
- **Evening** — no plans, gap block.

> **Book ahead:** Quinta da Pacheca tour+tasting+lunch — advance request/email or via

> **If it rains:** the whole anchor is under cover (tour, tasting room, restaurant) — no

> **Why this day works:** single anchor, the "long unhurried lunch over checkbox restaurant

### Day 8 — Friday 18 September — Empty, on purpose
**Base:** Lamego · **Drive:** none · **Pace:** light

- **Whole day** — nothing booked. Pool/terrace at the hotel, a slow walk if the mood strikes,
  last look at Régua or Lamego. **Optional, unscheduled add-ons if the mood strikes:** the
  Régua–Pinhão stretch of the scenic Linha do Douro train (the most beautiful stretch of the
  whole line, round trip returns to the same place so it doesn't complicate the car), or the
  São Salvador do Mundo miradouro dropped from Day 6 — both are genuine detours from this
  base, not "on the way" anywhere, so treat either as a real half-day commitment if chosen,
  not a quick add-on.
- This is explicitly the trip's second empty day, placed right before the long Gerês transfer
  so the transfer day isn't also the first rest in a week.

> **Book ahead:** nothing, unless the train is chosen — same-week booking is fine.
> **If it rains:** nothing to cancel; the train option is indoors regardless.
> **Why this day works:** "at least one empty day per week" times two for a 17-day trip, and it
> buffers the hardest travel day (Day 9) with a fully rested start.

### Day 9 — Saturday 19 September — Douro to Gerês (transfer only)
**Base:** Vila do Gerês · **Drive:** ~1h45–2h (sources disagree; treat as ~2h) · **Pace:** light —
**zero booked anchors, by explicit design**

- **Whole day** — this is the one leg in the trip that cannot be squeezed under the ~90-min
  travel-day guideline no matter how it's routed (confirmed by `research/logistics.md` against
  two independent estimates). Per the "drive time on a day > 2h: strong negative" scoring
  rule, this day is deliberately built with **no second commitment** — a café or miradouro stop
  roughly midway is fine and unscheduled, but nothing booked. Arrive Vila do Gerês, check in,
  easy evening.

> **Book ahead:** nothing.
> **If it rains:** doesn't matter, it's a driving day.
> **Why this day works:** honesty about drive time beats pretending a 2-hour transfer is a
> half-day — this is the explicit exception to the "under 90 min" rule, justified by there
> being no faster routing and no anchor stacked on top of it.

### Day 10 — Sunday 20 September — Waterfall walk
**Base:** Vila do Gerês · **Drive:** ~15–20 min each way to the Ermida trailhead ·
**Pace:** medium

- **Morning** — drive to Ermida, Trilho da Cascata do Arado / Poço Azul (~10.3 km
  out-and-back, moderate, half-day) — multi-drop granite waterfall. **Safety note from
  `research/nature.md`: do not swim at the Arado falls itself** (strong currents, slippery
  rocks) — the actual safe swim spot is **Poço Azul, a separate calmer pool ~9km along the
  trail**, reached by a slippery, uneven descent that needs sturdy shoes.
- **Lunch** — back in Vila do Gerês or packed.
- **Afternoon** — free; swim at Poço Azul specifically (not at the falls) if the water's
  inviting, or rest at the base — spa/pool in Vila do Gerês is the fallback since September
  water levels for wild swimming aren't confirmed.
- **Evening** — dedicated Minho dinner — rojões à Minhota or papas de sarrabulho, a real
  register-change from the Douro's wine-forward food.

> **Book ahead:** nothing required — no permit for this trail. A dinner reservation is a
> nice-to-have.
> **If it rains:** the trail becomes a wet granite walk, not a wash — but skip the swim
> regardless of destination, and treat the in-town spa/pool as the backup plan for the water
> element of the day. If it's a washout, shorten to the more tree-covered Trilho da Preguiça
> instead.
> **Why this day works:** single half-day anchor, real walk with a real payoff, built-in
> swim/spa backup, and the Minho meal placed against a lighter afternoon rather than stacked
> onto a second hike.
>
> **Date note:** today is **the last day** `research/nature.md`'s Mata de Albergaria toll
> booths are confirmed staffed in 2026 (25 June–20 September). This trail (Ermida/Arado) does
> not pass through Mata de Albergaria, so it's not affected — but if Ariel ever wants to swap
> in the Geira Romana walk (Portela do Homem→Vilarinho das Furnas) instead, today is the only
> day of the trip it would still be a staffed toll, not an unstaffed one.
>
> **Note on this cycle's change:** the previous draft gave Gerês 3 nights and a second full
> day (a Miradouro da Pedra Bela hike). This cycle trims Gerês to **2 nights** to keep the
> total trip at 17 days rather than 18 — see "Still soft." Pedra Bela is still a strong
> candidate to add back if a 3rd Gerês night is preferred over a day elsewhere.

### Day 11 — Monday 21 September — Back to Porto: the car, the port, a real dinner
**Base:** Porto (Cedofeita) · **Drive:** ~1h15 (the short exit leg, notably easier than the
inbound Douro→Gerês drive) · **Pace:** full (2 anchors, deliberately — see below)

- **Morning** — check out, drive back to Porto (the easy direction).
- **Midday** — return the rental car at **Campanha** (not the airport — there's no flight
  today, and this avoids an airport detour that no longer serves any purpose now the trip
  doesn't end in Porto). Check into Cedofeita, same neighbourhood as the arrival leg.
- **Afternoon** — Graham's or Taylor's port-house tour in Gaia (~1h) — the one Gaia house,
  deliberately placed here rather than on arrival or (as in a previous draft) a since-removed
  final Porto day: this is now the last point in the trip they'll be in Porto, so wine bought
  here rides in the campervan rather than needing to be hauled through the Douro and Gerês
  legs. `research/porto.md` recommends a **weekday morning** slot to dodge coach traffic — a
  Monday works for the weekday, but this itinerary's shape puts the visit in the afternoon
  since the Gerês drive comes first; worth revisiting if a specific morning slot matters more
  than the day's overall shape.
- **Evening** — the trip's "serious meal": The Yeatman (2-star splurge, a 10-minute walk from
  the Gaia port houses — pairs naturally with this afternoon) or Cantinho do Avillez in town
  if a more casual, easier-to-book option is preferred.

> **Book ahead:** Graham's/Taylor's tour slot (standard tours sell out 2–3 days ahead in
> September), and the chosen "serious meal" restaurant (weeks-out lead time in September).
> **If it rains:** the port-house tour is indoor regardless; irrelevant.
> **Why this day works:** this is a deliberate 2-anchor day (unlike most of the trip) because
> it's a one-time convergence point — car handover, the single Gaia visit, and the trip's one
> splurge dinner all make sense on the same day, and there's no drive afterward to worry about
> since tomorrow's departure (Day 12) starts fresh on foot before the van pickup. No rest gap
> today, but Day 8 and Day 13 sit close enough on either side to absorb it.
>
> **This also resolves last cycle's unaddressed Day 12 parking question**: because the
> Douro/Gerês rental car is now returned the same day it arrives back in Porto (not kept
> overnight before a later departure), there's no overnight rental-car parking cost to budget
> for. The only vehicle in Porto overnight tonight is nothing — the campervan isn't picked up
> until tomorrow morning.

### Day 12 — Tuesday 22 September — Van pickup, Aveiro, onto the coast
**Base:** Peniche/Baleal (campervan) · **Drive:** ~1h (Porto→Aveiro) + ~1h40–2h
(Aveiro→Baleal) · **Pace:** medium — one travel day, one light stop, no second booked anchor

- **Morning** — collect the campervan (Porto depot — company/exact location still open, see
  `trip/open-questions.md`), full walkthrough of water/power/gas systems before leaving the
  depot.
- **Midday** — drive to Aveiro (~1h), stop for a moliceiro canal cruise (~45 min) and a walk
  past the Art Nouveau buildings, then 15 minutes to Costa Nova for the striped houses.
  Treated as the half-day stop `research/coast.md` recommends, not a base — don't sleep here.
  Ovos moles as the local sweet to try.
- **Afternoon** — drive on to Peniche/Baleal (~1h40–2h, the longest single driving leg on the
  coastal side, comparable to the Porto→Pinhão Douro transfer). Figueira da Foz is skipped
  per `research/coast.md`'s read — a built-up resort stop that doesn't add character the other
  bases don't already give better.
- **Evening** — check into **ASA Peniche** (serviced motorhome area, €11–15/night) or an
  equivalent pitch near Baleal; short drive, not walk, from Baleal beach itself — settle in,
  casual dinner.

> **Book ahead:** the van rental itself (still needs a live September 2026 quote — see
> `trip/open-questions.md`), nothing else.
> **If it rains:** Aveiro's cruise and walk both tolerate light rain; if it's a washout, skip
> straight to the coastal drive.
> **Why this day works:** one real driving commitment split by a genuine half-day stop, not a
> second anchor stacked on top — mirrors how Day 3 and Day 6 treat transfer days honestly.
