- **Other one-way-capable operators worth quoting**: Yescapa (peer-to-peer, from

  pickup/drop address), Portugal by Van (Lisbon/Porto/Faro, one-way possible for a

  Sources: [Yescapa Portugal](https://www.yescapa.com/campervan-hire/portugal),
  [Portugal by Van](https://portugalbyvan.com/),
  [Camper Line Lisbon](https://www.rent-motorhome.com/rv-rental-center/rv-rental-center-camper-line-lisbon-lisboa)
- **Rough pricing**: general motorhome/campervan day rates in Portugal run
  **€50–220/day** depending on size and season; a week from Porto starts around
  **€600** off-season, rising well over **€1500/week** for larger Class C vans at
  peak summer. Late September is shoulder season, so expect the lower-to-mid end.
  `[likely]` One-way drop fees quoted elsewhere in Portugal run **€150–350**.
  `[unverified — specific to Porto→Lisbon]`
  Source: [rent-motorhome.com Porto pricing](https://www.rent-motorhome.com/blog/weekly-rv-rental-in-porto-pricing)
- **Fuel Porto→Lisbon direct (A1)**: ~€20–25. `[likely]` (Obviously this trip won't
  drive it direct — it's the floor, not the real cost.)
- **Licence**: a standard **category B licence covers vans up to 3,500 kg** (up to 9
  seats). Some countries extend B to 4,250 kg for noncommercial motorhome use if
  you've held the licence 3+ years — confirm this applies to whatever licence Ariel
  holds, but a normal rental campervan (not a large Class C motorhome) is comfortably
  under 3,500 kg and needs nothing special. `[likely]`
  Source: [CampingCar-Online driving rules Portugal](https://www.campingcar-online.com/en/mortorhome-rental-portugal/driving-driving-licence-a71.html)
- **Size on Portuguese coastal/village roads**: go smaller, not bigger. A compact
  campervan (VW-transporter-class, not a boxy Class C motorhome) is what people
  recommend for Portugal's narrow village streets and tight coastal parking —
  this also fits Baleal/Ericeira's small streets better. `[likely, general
  consensus across guides, no single citable number]`
- **Shower/toilet**: varies by model and is **not automatic** on compact campervans —
  many have only a chemical toilet and an outside/exterior shower, or neither, relying
  on campsite facilities instead. Full indoor shower+toilet is more common on larger
  (harder-to-drive, harder-to-park) vans. **This is a real trade-off to decide
  explicitly**: compact + campsite showers, or bigger + onboard bathroom.
  `[likely]` → flagged to open-questions.
  Source: [benecar.pt motorhome vs campervan](https://benecar.pt/en/news/motorhome-or-campervan-everything-you-need-to-know)

## Where you may legally sleep

- **Since 2021, Portugal restricts but does not ban overnight stays**: an
  IMT-approved motorhome/campervan may park overnight (up to 48h, same
  municipality) in ordinary public parking **without** camping behaviour — no
  awnings, no chairs/tables out, no cooking outside the vehicle. `[likely]`
- **Wild camping is explicitly banned** in Natura 2000 areas, protected areas, and
  land covered by Coastal Zone Management Plans (POOC) — which is a lot of the
  scenic Atlantic coastline itself, including stretches near Peniche/Ericeira.
  `[likely]`
- **Fines**: €60–300 for ordinary violations, rising to **€120–600** in protected
  coastal/Natura 2000 zones. `[likely]`
  Sources: [Campstar wild camping Portugal](https://www.campstar.com/trends/en/wild-camping-portugal/),
  [Vancowork wild camping laws 2025](https://www.vancowork.com/en/blogs/news/wild-camping-in-portugal-laws-2025),
  [OceanCamper wild camping rules/fines](https://oceancamper.com/wild-camping-portugal/)
- **Practical read for this trip**: don't plan on free beach-side wild camping at
  Baleal/Ericeira — those are exactly the popular coastal spots most likely to be
  enforced. Use **ASAs or paid campsites** instead. `[likely]`
- **Áreas de Serviço para Autocaravanas (ASAs)** found near the route:
  - **ASA Peniche**: 60 pitches, 27–43 m² each, water/electricity/grey-water dump/WiFi.
    €11/night (no power) or €15/night (with 4A electricity). `[likely]`
    Source: [ASA Peniche official](https://www.asapeniche.pt/en/home-en/)
  - **Ericeira**: a service point at the Intermarché supermarket (EN116 Km1) — water
    fill, waste dump, fuel and shopping, not a full overnight campsite experience.
    Also two private motorhome parks: **Park Mil Regos** and **Ericeira Camping**.
    `[likely]` Source: search aggregation, see Ericeira Camping below.
  - No ASA specific to Aveiro/Figueira da Foz turned up in this pass — worth a
    dedicated look next cycle if Aveiro gets an overnight rather than a day-stop.
    `[unverified]`
- **Apps**: Park4Night and similar are the standard way people find and verify these
  spots in real time (crowd-sourced reviews, exact coordinates) — worth having
  installed for the trip itself, not just for planning. `[likely]`
  Source: [Park4Night ASA Peniche listing](https://park4night.com/en/place/35954)

## Stop-by-stop

### Aveiro / Figueira da Foz
- **Aveiro**: genuinely worth a stop, but a **half-day is enough**, not a night,
  unless the schedule needs a break. A 45-minute moliceiro canal cruise, a walk past
  the Art Nouveau buildings, then 15 minutes' drive to **Costa Nova** for the
  striped houses (an hour's stroll covers it), and ovos moles as the local sweet to
  actually try. Consensus across guides: one day (more realistically **one
  half-day**) is enough to "get" Aveiro; it rewards a longer stay mainly if you want
  beach time at Costa Nova/Barra beyond the photo stop. `[likely]`
  Sources: [hungrypursuit — is Aveiro worth it](https://hungrypursuit.com/is-aveiro-worth-visiting/),
  [wheregoesrose — Aveiro things to do](https://www.wheregoesrose.com/things-to-do-aveiro-portugal/)
- **My opinion**: given the "few stops, not filling the coast" brief, Aveiro reads as
  a **worthwhile lunch/afternoon stop on the drive**, not a base. Don't sleep here.
- **Figueira da Foz**: has a genuinely huge urban beach (one of the widest in
  Europe) and a nice cliff walk at Cabo Mondego, but it's a fairly built-up resort
  town, not the "village people actually live in" character this trip wants. Guides
  frame it as a solid stop **between Lisbon and Porto to reset**, not a destination
  in itself. `[likely]`
  Source: [theworldtravelindex — is Figueira da Foz worth it](https://theworldtravelindex.com/en/europe/portugal/figueira-da-foz/is-figueira-da-foz-worth-visiting)
- **My opinion**: skip it or treat as an optional lunch stop only if the drive timing
  needs a break. It doesn't add something Aveiro, Baleal, or Ericeira don't already
  give better — and every night here is a night not spent at ≥2-night minimums
  elsewhere. **This matches the "few, good stops" brief: pick Aveiro (half-day),
  skip Figueira da Foz** unless logistics need a stopover.

### Peniche / Baleal
- Sleep **on Baleal itself or immediately adjacent** for beach walkability. Baleal
  Surf Camp area and the hostels around it put you 5 minutes from the sand.
  `[likely]` For the campervan specifically, **ASA Peniche** (see above) is the
  legal, serviced option nearest the surf; it's in Peniche town, a short drive
  (not walk) to Baleal itself — worth checking exact distance before locking this
  in. `[likely]`
  Sources: [TripAdvisor Baleal Surf Camp](https://www.tripadvisor.com/Hotel_Review-g2015006-d1547974-Reviews-or130-Baleal_Surf_Camp-Baleal_Peniche_Leiria_District_Central_Portugal.html)
- **Beaches**: Praia de Baleal / Praia do Baleal (town-side, gentle), **Prainha**
  (the sheltered, easiest wave in the whole Peniche area, good for just swimming
  too), and **Lagide** (rockier, more exposed, better for surfers than swimmers).
  Late September: still warm air, water in the high-teens °C (Atlantic — see surf
  section), fewer crowds than August. `[likely]`
- **Eating**: **Bar do Bruno** on Baleal beach — petiscos and sunset drinks, casual.
  **Prainha / Amigos do Baleal** — family-run, turbot and tuna, repeatedly called
  the best seafood in the area. `[likely]`
  Source: search aggregation of Peniche/Baleal restaurant guides
- **What the town is like**: Baleal is small, surf-culture, laid-back — a
  fishing-village-turned-surf-town rather than a resort. Good fit for an
  almost-nothing day. Peniche proper (the fishing port) is a short drive away for
  a change of scene and the ferry to the Berlenga islands if there's an appetite
  for a boat/snorkel day — not researched in depth this cycle, flagging as a
  possible addition. `[unverified — worth a follow-up]`

### Ericeira
- **Sleep near São Sebastião / Ribeira d'Ilhas** for the surf-reserve beaches.
  **Ericeira Camping & Bungalows** (Av. São Sebastião) is ~1 km from Ribeira
  d'Ilhas beach, with a viewpoint ~400 m away; if the van is parked here it's a
  short walk, not true beachfront, to the water. `[likely]`
  Source: [Ericeira Camping & Bungalows](https://reserving.com/hotels/europe/portugal/lisbon/ericeira/ericeira-camping-bungalows)
- Also present: **Park Mil Regos** (private motorhome park) and the **Intermarché
  service point** for water/waste/fuel if not staying at a full campsite.
  `[unverified — not independently confirmed this cycle]`
- **Beaches**: **Ribeira d'Ilhas** (the reserve's centerpiece, contest wave, sandy
  beach, café), **Praia do Norte**, **Praia de São Sebastião** (town beach, easiest
  walk from the village itself), **Foz do Lizandro** (beginner-friendly, see surf
  below).
- **Eating**: **Mar D'Areia** — local, pick your own fish. **Tasca do Joel** —
  traditional Portuguese, family-run, repeatedly the top local recommendation over
  more touristy options in the village centre. `[likely]`
  Source: search aggregation of Ericeira restaurant guides
- **What the town is like**: Ericeira proper is a real fishing town with a
  compact whitewashed centre — more "town" than Baleal, still walkable, still
  low-key by Portuguese coastal standards even as a UNESCO-recognized surf
  destination. Good fit for a genuine base (≥2 nights per facts.md).

### Into Lisbon
- Rental companies with Lisbon depots (Indie Campers, Roadsurfer, Siesta Campers,
  Camper Line, Portugal by Van) sit at or near the city / **Camper Line is ~24 km
  from LIS airport** with an airport transfer offered. Several will do a custom
  drop location/time (e.g. Anywhere Campers) for a fee. `[likely]`
  Sources: [Camper Line Lisbon](https://www.rent-motorhome.com/rv-rental-center/rv-rental-center-camper-line-lisbon-lisboa),
  [Siesta Campers Lisbon](https://www.siestacampers.com/locations/lisbon)
- **Recommendation to weigh, not decide here**: return the van the evening before
  the flight and spend the **last night in Lisbon itself** (real bed, real
  restaurant, no packing up a van at 5am for an international flight) rather than
  vanning it right up to departure. This also removes the risk of an off-hours
  drop-off scramble. Flagged as an open question — depends on flight time, which is
  still unconfirmed.

- **Bottom line for Ariel**: Prainha (Baleal) and Ribeira d'Ilhas / Foz do Lizandro
  (Ericeira) are the sane, repeatable morning options. **Supertubos, Coxos, and
  Crazy Left are explicitly not recommended** unless he already surfs at an expert
  level and knows it — these are injury-risk waves, not "let's see how it goes"
  waves. `[confirmed as a difficulty split by every source checked]`
- **Board rental won't be a blocker**: plenty of surf schools in both Baleal and
  Ericeira rent boards without lessons. Ericeira sample pricing: **soft-top beginner
  board + wetsuit ~€15/day**, shortboard ~€20/day, high-performance board ~€30/day;
  half-day board alone ~€16, wetsuit ~€10. `[likely]` Assume Baleal is similar —
  not separately itemized this cycle. `[unverified for Baleal specifically]`
  Source: [Extra Surf School rentals](https://www.extrasurfschool.com/surf-rentals)
- **Late-September conditions**: not independently re-verified numerically this
  cycle (prior general knowledge: water ~17–19°C, 3/2mm or 4/3mm wetsuit territory,
  swell still active into autumn, crowds noticeably lower than August).
  `[unverified — flag for next cycle if it matters for gear decisions]`
